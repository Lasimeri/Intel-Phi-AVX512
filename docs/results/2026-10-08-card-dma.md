# 2026-10-08: the card pulls its activations with its own DMA engine

The request at one token costs about 0.25 ms, and the pull of its
activations (a few kilobytes read by the card's cores through the
uncached window, one PCIe round trip a 64-byte line) is 0.07 to 0.10 ms
of it (`2026-10-07-drafting-and-card-requests.md`). The card's SBOX DMA
engine can start transfers from the card side (SSDG 328207-002,
2.1.8.2.1). This record is the measurement of a card-owned channel driven
from the worker's own process, before and after it carries the pull.

Everything ran on **card 3** only (cards 0 to 2 served the decode
server, `PHI_GGML_CARDS=0,1,2`, untouched) of the GPU rack (EPYC 7742,
CachyOS, kernel 7.2.6-1-cachyos; the card's kernel the stack's). Logs
and scripts: `/mnt/raid5/phi/bench/dma-2026-10-08/` on the rack
(`bench.sh`, `before.sh`, `ab.sh`, `full.sh` and their outputs).

## The route: user space, channel 7

`card/vpu/cdma.md` has the whole design. In short: the channel's register
page through `/dev/mem` (uncached), the window's card address from the
ring region's header, the descriptor ring, status word and landing area in
one locked 2 MiB huge page whose physical address comes from
`/proc/self/pagemap`, one owner by `flock`. Chosen over a kernel device
because it needs no kernel change: the production cards take a new worker
with a worker restart, where a kernel patch would mean rebooting them. What
a kernel device would add is named in `cdma.md` (a truly pinned buffer,
the channel quiesced when its owner dies).

Which channel: `phictl` takes channels 0 and 1 from the host (the disk and
host-memory services; card 3's console log: "DMA channel 0 ... DCR
0x1557", "DMA channel 1 ... DCR 0x155f"), the card kernel's patches use
none, and MPSS gave the card channels 4 to 7 (`__LAST_HOST_CHAN_NUM` 3 in
`mic_dma_md.h`). DCR read 0x155F on card 3: channel 7's bits 00 (card
owned, disabled), and its registers all zero (DCAR, DHPR, DTPR, DRAR,
DSTAT, DCHERR, DCHERRMSK at SBOX 0xA1C0..0xA1F0, read with busybox
`devmem`): never used. Channel 7 it is.

## The standalone measurement

`scripts/phi-vpu.sh -c 3 dmabench 1000` (here through `bench.sh`, the
worker stopped first): `card/vpu/dmabench.md` describes each copy and the
checks. Medians and 99th percentiles in microseconds:

| bytes | dma | of it the submit | copy 1 thread | copy split (threads) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64 | 1.92 / 2.91 | 0.30 | 0.79 / 1.54 | 6.41 / 11.68 (1) |
| 4096 | 3.17 / 4.07 | 0.31 | 49.30 / 75.90 | 39.06 / 58.70 (2) |
| 5120 | 3.49 / 4.32 | 0.31 | 61.89 / 87.22 | 46.50 / 67.77 (2) |
| 8192 | 4.30 / 5.01 | 0.32 | 99.60 / 128.61 | 39.12 / 59.87 (4) |
| 16384 | 6.64 / 18.20 | 0.36 | 199.32 / 232.60 | 39.16 / 60.94 (8) |
| 65536 | 20.31 / 33.44 | 0.49 | 827.06 / 964.42 | 41.82 / 70.70 (32) |

- 0 wrong words in 6000 repetitions of each copy, every repetition a new
  pattern and a destination poisoned by the copying core's own stores
  (its lines dirty in that core's cache): the engine's writes into card
  memory are coherent with the cores' caches.
- Cross-core: 250 repetitions at 4 and 64 KiB poisoned on core 56,
  verified on core 10 and again on core 56: 0 and 0.
- 6501 copies by the engine; the 256-entry ring wrapped about 100 times,
  so reused descriptor slots were read correctly (descriptor reads are
  coherent too).
- Control: descriptors written, doorbell not rung, 50 ms: the wait
  returned 0 with error 9 (timeout) and all 512 words untouched.
- The engine costs about 2 us fixed plus about 3.2 GB/s. The worker's
  split copy is flat at 39 to 46 us whatever the size, because each copier
  reads its 32 lines one round trip after another (0.76 us a line, the
  one-thread column). At Flash Next's float16 activation row (5120 bytes)
  the engine is 13 times faster than the split copy.
- Seen on the way: after the channel is disabled its tail register reads
  0 (DSTAT 0x20000000); the open puts the head at whatever tail it finds.

## The request after (card 3, the worker with the channel)

`worker.S` opens channel 7 at start and `matmul.S` pulls each request's
ids and activations with it, in one copy into the channel's landing area,
submitted and waited for by the dispatcher before the ids check
(`card/vpu/cdma.md`, "In the worker"; `-d 0` leaves the channel closed).
`ab.sh`: one binary started with the channel open (`-d 1`) and closed
(`-d 0`), interleaved over two rounds (this host drifts), each round the
q6_K check at `--repeat 5` and then the one-token shapes above at 60
repetitions, float16 activations, 114 threads. Milliseconds, round 1,
median of 60 (best); round 2 within 0.011 ms of it on every cell:

- The card's total at the median falls 16, 23, 11, 18 and 18 percent,
  the host's round trip 14, 21, 12, 17 and 19. `before.sh` run once more
  on the worker as committed (`final.out`, two rounds) reads the same:
  card total 0.193 / 0.190, 0.388 / 0.389, 0.285 / 0.282, 0.143 / 0.143
  and 0.172 / 0.182 at the median. The pull column is now
  the engine's copy, submitted and waited for (5 to 11 us: about 3.5 of
  it the copy of a 5 KiB row, the rest the descriptor and the checks
  before it); the compute column reads higher than before (0.162 against
  0.129 ms at one row) because it now spans the whole dispatch (the
  pool's wake, the groups, the weight warm-up, the rows), where a fused
  request's compute was counted from the last copy's completion. What
  the sum says: the copy the first cores made inside the dispatch, and
  the wait of the other 110 threads for it, were 30 to 45 us of a
  one-token request, and they are gone.
- The stage trace (`after-worker.log`, the same shapes under
  `before.sh`'s run on this worker; means over 60): a mixture request
  reads 0.8 us, descriptors and checks 2.0 to 2.5, **pull 6.1 to 8.5**
  (the ids copy was 2.7 to 2.9 before, with the row's copy inside the
  dispatch), groups 2.5 to 2.9, **compute 141 to 189** (181 to 233
  before), push 15 to 25, reply 1.1; gate and up as one request 272 to
  283 of compute (311 to 326); the dense column 162 (213).
- Nothing changes at a prompt's size: the q6_K check's weight rates at
  4096 x 5120 (`--repeat 5`, rounds 1 and 2) are 0.423 / 0.411 ms at n 1
  with the channel open against 0.406 / 0.395 closed, 1.093 / 1.070
  against 1.061 / 1.083 at n 8, and 8.323 / 8.309 against 8.351 / 8.275
  at n 64, where 655 KiB of activations go through the landing area;
  the fused feed-forward's rows within 0.3 ms either way. (A first run
  at `--repeat 1` had read 12.4 against 10.1 ms at n 64: the first
  request's touch, not the engine.)
- **Results are the same bytes.** `full.sh`: `matmul-check` whole
  (every type, both groupings, up to four matrices in one request, the
  fused feed-forward, float16 activations, 114 threads) on this worker
  and on the one built from `c42f078`, their outputs compared with the

  every case `ok`; and the same with float32 activations on this worker.
  The q6_K and q8_0 checks of `before.sh` and the after-run read the
  same after the strip as well.
- Every start of the day after the first found the channel enabled and
  idle, left by the worker `phi-vpu.sh` had killed (no `cdma_close` on
  SIGTERM), and took it over: the worker's log line reads `DCR found
  0x955f, tail 0` (enabled; the tail reads 0 once the open has disabled
  it, as `dmabench` saw, so the head goes to 0), then the nonce copy
  verified (`status word 1`); the copies after that were the ones the
  identity check saw. A worker started between two with the channel
  (`full.sh`'s `c42f078` worker) leaves it as it found it.

## What deploying to cards 0 to 2 takes

The worker binary only: `scripts/phi-vpu.sh -c N deploy`, then a worker
restart (`start` with the backend's arguments, `PHI_VPU_HUGEPAGES=2400`,
`PHI_VPU_ARGS="-e 0"`), which loses the uploads the running backend
made, so the decode server that holds the cards restarts with it. No
kernel change, no card reboot: the channel is programmed from user
space, and the card kernel's patches do not touch it. No change to the
host (`libggml_phi.so` and the daemon are as they were): the protocol
is unchanged, the nonce line at 128 is the card's alone, and the
engine's channel 7 is one the host never took (`phictl` holds 0 and 1).
Not deployed there by this record: cards 0 to 2 were serving.

## Next

The push (results to the window, 13 to 27 us of a one-token request) is
the same engine the other way: a copy into host memory with its status
word in host memory too (the host's `dma.md` on why), read back by an
uncached load. And the dispatcher could dispatch before the copy lands
and let the threads wait on the status word, with the ids checked by
each thread, which would hide the remaining 3.5 us under the pool's wake.
