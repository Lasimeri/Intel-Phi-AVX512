# 2026-10-08: the card pulls its activations with its own DMA engine

The request at one token costs about 0.25 ms, and the pull of its
activations (a few kilobytes read by the card's cores through the
uncached window, one PCIe round trip a 64-byte line) is 0.07 to 0.10 ms
of it (`2026-10-07-drafting-and-card-requests.md`). The card's SBOX DMA
engine can start transfers from the card side (SSDG 328207-002,
2.1.8.2.1). This record is the measurement of a card-owned channel driven
from the worker's own process, before and after it carries the pull.

Everything ran on **card 3** only (cards 0 to 2 served the decode
server, `PHI_GGML_CARDS=0,1,2`, untouched) of the GPU rack (EPYC 7742,
CachyOS, kernel 7.2.6-1-cachyos; the card's kernel the stack's). Logs
and scripts: `/mnt/raid5/phi/bench/dma-2026-10-08/` on the rack
(`bench.sh`, `before.sh`, `ab.sh`, `full.sh` and their outputs).

## The route: user space, channel 7

`card/vpu/cdma.md` has the whole design. In short: the channel's register
page through `/dev/mem` (uncached), the window's card address from the
ring region's header, the descriptor ring, status word and landing area in
one locked 2 MiB huge page whose physical address comes from
`/proc/self/pagemap`, one owner by `flock`. Chosen over a kernel device
because it needs no kernel change: the production cards take a new worker
with a worker restart, where a kernel patch would mean rebooting them. What
a kernel device would add is named in `cdma.md` (a truly pinned buffer,
the channel quiesced when its owner dies).

Which channel: `phictl` takes channels 0 and 1 from the host (the disk and
host-memory services; card 3's console log: "DMA channel 0 ... DCR
0x1557", "DMA channel 1 ... DCR 0x155f"), the card kernel's patches use
none, and MPSS gave the card channels 4 to 7 (`__LAST_HOST_CHAN_NUM` 3 in
`mic_dma_md.h`). DCR read 0x155F on card 3: channel 7's bits 00 (card
owned, disabled), and its registers all zero (DCAR, DHPR, DTPR, DRAR,
DSTAT, DCHERR, DCHERRMSK at SBOX 0xA1C0..0xA1F0, read with busybox
`devmem`): never used. Channel 7 it is.

## The standalone measurement

`scripts/phi-vpu.sh -c 3 dmabench 1000` (here through `bench.sh`, the
worker stopped first): `card/vpu/dmabench.md` describes each copy and the
checks. Medians and 99th percentiles in microseconds:

| bytes | dma | of it the submit | copy 1 thread | copy split (threads) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64 | 1.92 / 2.91 | 0.30 | 0.79 / 1.54 | 6.41 / 11.68 (1) |
| 4096 | 3.17 / 4.07 | 0.31 | 49.30 / 75.90 | 39.06 / 58.70 (2) |
| 5120 | 3.49 / 4.32 | 0.31 | 61.89 / 87.22 | 46.50 / 67.77 (2) |
| 8192 | 4.30 / 5.01 | 0.32 | 99.60 / 128.61 | 39.12 / 59.87 (4) |
| 16384 | 6.64 / 18.20 | 0.36 | 199.32 / 232.60 | 39.16 / 60.94 (8) |
| 65536 | 20.31 / 33.44 | 0.49 | 827.06 / 964.42 | 41.82 / 70.70 (32) |

- 0 wrong words in 6000 repetitions of each copy, every repetition a new
  pattern and a destination poisoned by the copying core's own stores
  (its lines dirty in that core's cache): the engine's writes into card
  memory are coherent with the cores' caches.
- Cross-core: 250 repetitions at 4 and 64 KiB poisoned on core 56,
  verified on core 10 and again on core 56: 0 and 0.
- 6501 copies by the engine; the 256-entry ring wrapped about 100 times,
  so reused descriptor slots were read correctly (descriptor reads are
  coherent too).
- Control: descriptors written, doorbell not rung, 50 ms: the wait
  returned 0 with error 9 (timeout) and all 512 words untouched.
- The engine costs about 2 us fixed plus about 3.2 GB/s. The worker's
  split copy is flat at 39 to 46 us whatever the size, because each copier
  reads its 32 lines one round trip after another (0.76 us a line, the
  one-thread column). At Flash Next's float16 activation row (5120 bytes)
  the engine is 13 times faster than the split copy.
- Seen on the way: after the channel is disabled its tail register reads
  0 (DSTAT 0x20000000); the open puts the head at whatever tail it finds.

## The request before (card 3, the worker built from c42f078)

`before.sh`: the worker from the repository head in `/opt/phi/vpu-base`,
started with the stage trace (`-v -e 0 -t 60`, 2400 huge pages, 114
threads), `matmul-check` for q6_K and q8_0 (both pass), then the one-token
shapes of the 2026-10-07 record at float16 activations, 60 repetitions,
two rounds. Milliseconds, best (median):

| request | compute | pull | push | card total | host round trip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 160 x 2560, one row (Q6_K) | 0.119 (0.129) | 0.076 | 0.013 | 0.202 (0.225) | 0.206 (0.230) |
| gate and up as two requests | 0.241 (0.262) | 0.156 | 0.027 | 0.422 (0.497) | 0.429 (0.506) |
| gate and up as one request | 0.164 (0.179) | 0.106 | 0.025 | 0.300 (0.321) | 0.304 (0.326) |
| 576 x 512, eight rows (Q8_0) | 0.069 (0.075) | 0.069 | 0.023 | 0.162 (0.176) | 0.168 (0.181) |
| 2048 x 2560, plain, one column (Q8_0) | 0.086 (0.093) | 0.096 | 0.018 | 0.197 (0.215) | 0.201 (0.220) |

(Second round within 0.008 ms of the first.) The pull of a fused request
is timed from the request's start to the last copier's count, so it
holds the descriptor reads, the ids, the group building, the pool's wake
and the weight warm-up as well as the copy; the stage trace splits a
mixture request (means over 60): read 0.9 us, descriptors and checks 2.0
to 2.2, ids 2.7 to 2.9, groups 3.3, then the compute dispatch 210 to 233
(the fused copy inside it), push 14 to 18, reply 1.3 to 1.4; the pool's
trace has the last thread seeing a dispatch 9 to 25 us after it starts.
