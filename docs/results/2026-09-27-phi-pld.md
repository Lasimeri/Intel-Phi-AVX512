# 2026-09-27: phi-pld, prompt lookup decoding of this repository's own, on the cards

> **Read first.** The tokens per second here are of prompt lookup on a
> *copy* task (177 lines of Rust rewritten with one rename), measured on
> the **Q4_K_M**, and count the **generation phase only**; the 2385-token
> prompt took another 52 to 72 s, so the whole requests ran at 4.2 to 6.5
> tokens per second. **Writing code from a description runs at about 8
> tokens per second** (the Q8_0 on both cards), and a 2B draft model
> makes that slower: `2026-09-27-code-generation-q8.md`.

Host: Ryzen 7 5800X, 31 GiB, kernel 7.2.6-1-cachyos, both cards up
(workers at 114 threads). llama.cpp `build-native` at f5b9bd3, unchanged
and used as a library. The record before this one
(`2026-09-27-prompt-lookup-llama-server.md`) measured llama-server's own
n-gram drafters with the cards; this one builds the engine the two
sources describe, in Rust over libllama (`host/crates/phi-pld`), and
measures it against them:

- Apoorv Saxena's prompt lookup decoding
  (https://github.com/apoorvumang/prompt-lookup-decoding): the context's
  last n tokens, n from 3 down to 1, found earlier in the context, and up
  to 10 of the tokens that followed them there as the draft;
- Hayder Tirmazi's data structures for llama.cpp's n-gram caches
  (https://jadidbourbaki.github.io/blog/prompt-lookup-llama-cpp/): flat
  open-addressing tables in place of nested maps, nothing copied per step.

Greedy, served as llama-server serves (`/completion`,
`/v1/chat/completions`, its timing fields), one request at a time.

## Method

Qwen3.8-35B-A3B Q4_K_M offloaded to both cards (`PHI_GGML_OFFLOAD=1`,
`scripts/phi-ggml.sh`), `-t 12 -c 8192`, batch and micro-batch 512,
llama-server with `--no-repack -np 1 --reasoning off`, phi-pld with its
repacking off (the same). The three prompts of the llama-server record
(built from this repository: a 177-line code edit, a table rewritten as
a list, a free answer about the key-value cache), rendered once by
llama-server's `/apply-template` (2385, 1692 and 39 tokens), sent to
`/completion` of each engine: 400 tokens at most, `temperature 0`,
`return_tokens`, so the outputs compare token for token. Each engine a
fresh process, one at a time (one process holds the cards), alternated;
rates are each engine's `timings.predicted_per_second`, resident memory
the server's `VmRSS` after the three requests.

## Rolling back a hybrid model: checkpoints, not snapshots

This model keeps a recurrent state beside its attention cache, which
cannot be truncated like the cache. llama.cpp offers two ways to take a
rejected draft back: snapshots of the state for the last few tokens
(`n_rs_seq`, experimental), then truncation; or a checkpoint of the state
before the batch (`llama_state_seq_get_data_ext`, partial), restored on a
rejection, then the kept tokens decoded again (llama-server's way with its
n-gram drafters). `phi-pld verify-cost` times a decode of 1 + k tokens
with every row's choice read (median of 5):

| k | no snapshots | 4 snapshots | 49 snapshots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 117 ms | 109 | 130 |
| 2 | 153 | 191 | 214 |
| 4 | 185 | 249 | 278 |
| 8 | 275 | 341 | 441 |
| 16 | 494 | 513 | 771 |
| 32 | 801 | | 1348 |
| 48 | 1084 | 1107 | 1906 |

The snapshots cost on every verification, 17 ms per token at 49 and
still 38 ms at k = 2 with 4; a checkpoint is 66 MB and 10 ms to save, 10
to restore. So phi-pld keeps none (`--rs-seq 0`) and checkpoints.

And it does not decode the kept tokens again on their own: after the
restore they wait, and ride in front of the next step's batch, whose rows
are read from the next token on (a column costs about 20 ms, a pass of
its own about 117). The checkpoint still holds the state before them, so
a rejection there restores it again; past 8 tokens in the batch they are
decoded on their own first (`host/crates/phi-pld/src/decode.md`). With
every draft wrong (the hidden `--junk`), generation went from 3.34, 3.49
and 3.71 tokens per second to 3.83, 3.95 and 4.26.

## Choosing the drafter by simulation

A drafted token the model rejects costs a column of a verification
batch, and on a mixture of experts a column is not free: each brings
about eight experts' weights into the batch. What a drafter's options
are worth depends on how often each kind of draft is accepted, so
`phi-pld simulate` runs the engine itself (the same `generate`) against a
replay of the model's drafting-off output: the replay answers each
decode with what the model chose, checks that every decode continues
the context and that no rejected token was left in the state, and prices
each call from the table above (0.28 s a run,
`host/crates/phi-pld/src/sim.md`). A sweep of 320 and then 192 option
sets over the three prompts (speedup over plain generation, modelled):

| options | code edit | extraction | free answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the original's: n-grams 1 to 3, 10 tokens, fixed | 3.66 | 0.96 | 0.52 |
| n-grams 3 to 12, 2 to 64 tokens, adapting (the default) | 5.04 | 1.54 | 1.00 |
| the same, `--fold-max 0` (carried tokens decoded on their own before a draft) | 5.04 | 1.44 | 1.00 |

The original's 1-token matches are what loses: on the free answer 1224
tokens drafted after a 1-gram, 15 accepted. Adapting the length (double
after a draft taken whole, down to what was accepted plus one after a
rejection) and requiring 3 tokens of match win on every prompt. The
simulation reads about 10 percent high against the measured runs below
(the code edit 5.04 against 4.2, the original's free answer 0.52 against
0.47): it cannot see the batched kernels' rounding or the time outside
the decodes.

none, phi-pld defaults, 12/48. phi-pld with drafting off generated at
9.47, 9.90 and 11.11 earlier in the day, as llama-server does.

- **The copy**: phi-pld and llama-server's 12/48 are level (about 40,
  4.2 times plain), both accepting every draft.
- **The extraction**: phi-pld 13.1, 1.32 times plain and 43 percent past
  llama-server's best (12/16, 9.19), where 12/48 loses 27 percent to
  plain: phi-pld drafts less than half the tokens 12/48 does and has more than
  twice as many accepted, and its rejections cost a restore and a few
  carried columns instead of a pass.
- **The free answer**: 3 percent under plain (12 short drafts, 5 tokens
  accepted); llama-server's drafters find nothing to draft there.
- **The prompt**: phi-pld reads the code edit's 2385-token prompt 25 to
  40 percent slower than llama-server (64.9 and 72.4 s against 51.7 and
  51.5, the same batches), so for the whole request it is slower than
  llama-server's 12/48 (5.37 and 4.82 tokens per second against 6.47 and

- **Memory**: phi-pld holds 0.5 GB less of the host than llama-server;
  both peak at 21.1 GB while loading, before the cards' rows leave.
- **The original's options** lose half the speed on the free answer, as
  the simulation said.

## Verified

- Drafting off, phi-pld's tokens are llama-server's exactly on all three
  prompts (once the prompt is decoded in llama-server's batches, which on
  a model whose state it checkpoints end 512 and 4 tokens before the
  prompt's end: `prompt_cuts`).
- With every draft wrong, so that every step restores and carries, the
  code edit's and the extraction's tokens are drafting-off's exactly (397
  and 392 restores).
- With the default drafting, the code edit's tokens are drafting-off's
  exactly; the extraction's part at token 100 and the free answer's at
  token 9. `phi-pld margin` decodes the drafting-off output one token at a
  time to that point and shows the choices there: at the extraction's,
  " **" 22.26 against " " 21.79, the third 7.4 lower; at the free
  answer's, " (" 21.98 against " cache" 21.95, the third 7.1 lower. Each
  is a two-way near tie that the verification batch's rounding moved (the
  run took the second), as llama-server's own drafters do to this answer
  (the record before this one). A fault in the rollback would not pick the
  runner-up of a near tie and then continue fluently; the all-rejected
  runs above rule it out.
- The replay's tests drive every rollback path (attention-only and
  recurrent, 0, 4 and 64 snapshots, fixed and adapting drafts, carried
  tokens folded or flushed, every draft rejected) and check the output
  each time (`cargo test -p phi-pld`).

## The backend's side

A verification column costs about 20 ms with the cards, against 14.5 ms
per token at a 512-token prompt: at 17 columns a batch touches about a
hundred of the 256 experts of every layer, and the host reads the rows of
them it keeps. That is where a drafter's cost sits, and it is the
backend's next target.
